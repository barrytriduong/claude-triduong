Someone asks you a question in English. You hear it, translate it into your language, think of an answer, translate the answer back into English, and finally speak. By then, the conversation has moved on.

Translating is natural when you start learning. But at some point, it becomes the thing that slows you down. The goal isn't to stop using your first language completely. It's to build a direct path between English words and your ideas. These seven habits help you build it.

## 1. Narrate your day

As you do simple things, describe them in your head: *"I'm making coffee. The water is too hot. I'm late again."* Keep it simple. You're not writing a book, you're training your brain to reach for English first.

## 2. Use the words you already know

When you don't know a word, don't stop to translate. Describe it instead: *"the thing you use to open a bottle"*. Native speakers do this all the time, and it is a real speaking skill.

## 3. Switch to an English-English dictionary

Look up new words in a learner's dictionary written *in English*. Reading definitions in English keeps you thinking in English, and you learn extra words for free.

## 4. Think in chunks, not single words

Fluent speakers don't build sentences one word at a time. They use ready-made **chunks**: *"to be honest"*, *"it depends on"*, *"I'm not sure yet"*. When you learn a new word, learn the chunk it lives in. Our lessons always show the sentence from the video for this reason.

## 5. Talk to yourself, out loud

It may feel silly, but speaking to yourself is safe practice with no pressure. Rehearse a conversation before a meeting, or retell a story you watched today. The more you say things, the more automatic they become.

## 6. Change one thing in your daily life to English

Pick one: your phone language, your music, the news you read, or the videos you watch while eating. Small, daily contact adds up faster than a big study session once a week.

## 7. Keep a one-line diary

Every evening, write **one sentence** in English about your day. Just one. After a month you'll have thirty sentences, and you'll notice that they come faster and sound more natural.

## Be patient with yourself

Thinking in English doesn't happen in a day. First it happens with simple thoughts, then with longer ones. Every time you catch yourself thinking in English, even for a few seconds, that's real progress.
