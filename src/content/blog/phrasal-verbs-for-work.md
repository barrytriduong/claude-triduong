Native speakers use phrasal verbs all the time at work. Instead of *"postpone the meeting"*, they say *"put off the meeting"*. Instead of *"investigate the problem"*, they say *"look into it"*. If you learn the most common ones, your English will sound much more natural, and you'll understand your colleagues more easily.

Here are 25 of the most useful, grouped by situation.

## Meetings

| Phrasal verb | Meaning | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **set up** | arrange or organise | Can you **set up** a meeting with the design team for Friday? |
| **bring up** | mention a topic | I'd like to **bring up** the budget before we finish. |
| **put off** | postpone, move to later | We've **put off** the launch until next month. |
| **call off** | cancel | The client **called off** the meeting at the last minute. |
| **go over** | review or check carefully | Let's **go over** the plan one more time. |

## Tasks and projects

| Phrasal verb | Meaning | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **take on** | accept a job or responsibility | She **took on** two new projects this year. |
| **carry out** | do or complete (a task, plan, survey) | We **carried out** a survey of 500 customers. |
| **come up with** | think of (an idea or plan) | The team **came up with** a great name for the app. |
| **hand in** | submit (a report, form or work) | Please **hand in** your reports by Thursday. |
| **fill in for** | do someone's job while they're away | Can you **fill in for** Tom while he's on holiday? |
| **run out of** | have no more of something | We're **running out of** time, so let's decide now. |

## Problems

| Phrasal verb | Meaning | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **look into** | investigate | I'll **look into** the problem and let you know. |
| **sort out** | solve or organise | Don't worry, IT will **sort out** your laptop. |
| **figure out** | understand or find the answer | We need to **figure out** why sales dropped. |
| **deal with** | take action on a problem or person | He's great at **dealing with** difficult customers. |
| **work out** | find a solution; calculate | We **worked out** a plan that suits everyone. |
| **back up** | support someone; copy files for safety | Thanks for **backing me up** in the meeting. / Always **back up** your files. |

## Emails and communication

| Phrasal verb | Meaning | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **get back to** | reply to someone later | I'll **get back to** you by the end of the day. |
| **follow up (on)** | contact again or continue with something | I'm writing to **follow up on** our call yesterday. |
| **point out** | tell someone something important | She **pointed out** a mistake in the contract. |
| **catch up (with)** | talk to get the latest news | Let's **catch up** after lunch about the project. |
| **keep up with** | stay at the same level or speed | It's hard to **keep up with** all these emails! |

## People and jobs

| Phrasal verb | Meaning | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **turn down** | refuse (an offer or request) | He **turned down** the job because of the long hours. |
| **step down** | leave an important position | The CEO **stepped down** after ten years. |
| **lay off** | end people's jobs because there isn't enough work | The factory **laid off** 200 workers. |

## How to learn them

Don't try to learn all 25 today. Choose **five** that are useful for *your* job, and:

1. Write your own example sentence about your real work.
2. Say each one out loud three times.
3. Use one in a real email or meeting this week.
4. Review them on day 3 and day 7. Here's [why that works](/blog/spaced-repetition-remember-words/).

**Watch out for separable phrasal verbs.** With many of them, the object can go in the middle: *"set up a meeting"* or *"set a meeting up"*. But if the object is a pronoun, it **must** go in the middle: *"set it up"*, not ~~"set up it"~~.
