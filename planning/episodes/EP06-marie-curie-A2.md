# EP06 — Marie Curie: The Secret School and Two Nobel Prizes

- **Level:** A2 · **Length:** about 4½–5 minutes · **18 scenes** (standard format, as EP03)
- **5 video words:** prize · scientist · discover · element · university
- **Target structure:** *both … and* — "She won prizes in both physics and chemistry."
  (In the story 3 times: scenes 10, 12 and 15, plus the structure part of scene 7 and the review.)
- **Lesson extras (website only):** physics · chemistry · husband
- **Lesson page (after upload):** https://brightlivesenglish.com/lessons/marie-curie-two-nobel-prizes/

- **Voice:** same as EP03–EP05, every scene starts with `[slowly]`. Music quiet.
- **Visual rule:** stylized 3D animated storybook style only. No realistic images of Marie or Pierre Curie. Show them from behind, from the side at a distance, or small in a wide shot. No face close-ups. Every prompt ends with "No text. Stylized, not photorealistic."

## Words and structure (on-screen text)

| # | Word | Part of speech | Meaning (A2) | Example |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **prize** | noun | something you get when you win or do something very well | She won a Nobel Prize. |
| 2 | **scientist** | noun | a person who studies the world to learn how it works | Marie was a great scientist. |
| 3 | **discover** | verb (past: discovered) | to find something for the first time | She discovered two new elements. |
| 4 | **element** | noun | one of the simple things that everything is made of, like gold or iron | Radium is an element. |
| 5 | **university** | noun | a place where you study after school | Women couldn't go to university in Poland. |

**Structure (end of scene 7):**
> **both … and** — two things together
> *She studied physics. She studied mathematics.* → *She studied **both** physics **and** mathematics.*
> *She won prizes in **both** physics **and** chemistry.*

## Voiceover script (paste into Vids exactly, one block per scene)

**Scene 1 — Hook** (hook visuals only, no title text)
[slowly] In her country, girls couldn't go to university. So she studied at a secret school. Years later, she won two Nobel Prizes. This is the story of Marie Curie.

**Scene 2 — Welcome** (logo + tagline, as EP03)
[slowly] Hello, and welcome to Bright Lives English. Real stories. Real English. Today's level is A2. In this story, listen for five new words.

**Scene 3 — Word 1: prize** (on screen: *prize · noun · something you get when you win*)
[slowly] Prize. A prize is something you get when you win, or when you do something very well. She won a Nobel Prize.

**Scene 4 — Word 2: scientist** (on screen: *scientist · noun · a person who studies how the world works*)
[slowly] Scientist. A scientist is a person who studies the world to learn how it works. Marie was a great scientist.

**Scene 5 — Word 3: discover** (on screen: *discover · verb · to find something for the first time* · past: discovered)
[slowly] Discover. When you discover something, you find it for the first time. The past is discovered. She discovered two new elements.

**Scene 6 — Word 4: element** (on screen: *element · noun · one of the simple things that everything is made of*)
[slowly] Element. An element is one of the simple things that everything is made of, like gold or iron. Radium is an element.

**Scene 7 — Word 5: university + structure** (on screen: *university · noun · a place where you study after school*; then the structure box)
[slowly] University. A university is a place where you study after school. Now, one pattern: both… and. It puts two things together. She won prizes in both physics and chemistry. Ready? Let's begin.

**Scene 8 — A girl who loved books** (key word: **university** — disclaimer at the bottom: *Illustrations are artistic interpretations, not real photos of Marie Curie.*)
[slowly] Maria Skłodowska was born in Warsaw, Poland, in 1867. She loved books and learning. But at that time, women couldn't study at university in Poland.

**Scene 9 — The secret school** (key word: **university**)
[slowly] So Maria studied at a secret school called the Flying University. For about five years, she worked as a teacher for rich families. She saved money and helped her sister study in Paris.

**Scene 10 — Paris** (key word: **both … and**)
[slowly] In 1891, Maria moved to Paris and went to university. She had very little money. She was often cold and hungry. But she studied both physics and mathematics, and she was one of the best students.

**Scene 11 — Pierre** (key word: **scientist**)
[slowly] In Paris, she met a French scientist named Pierre Curie. They loved science, and they loved each other. They got married in 1895. Now she was Marie Curie.

**Scene 12 — The shed** (key word: **discovered**)
[slowly] Both Marie and Pierre worked in an old, cold shed. They worked day and night. In 1898, they discovered two new elements. They called one polonium, after Poland, and the other radium.

**Scene 13 — The first prize** (key word: **prize**)
[slowly] In 1903, Marie and Pierre won the Nobel Prize in physics, with another scientist, Henri Becquerel. Marie was the first woman in history to win a Nobel Prize.

**Scene 14 — Alone** (key word: **university**)
[slowly] In 1906, Pierre died in a street accident. Marie was very sad, but she didn't stop working. She became the first woman to teach as a professor at her university in Paris.

**Scene 15 — The second prize** (key word: **both … and**)
[slowly] In 1911, Marie won a second Nobel Prize, this time in chemistry. Today, she is still the only person with Nobel Prizes in both physics and chemistry.

**Scene 16 — Review**
[slowly] Let's review. She won a Nobel Prize. Marie was a great scientist. She discovered two new elements. Radium is an element. And women couldn't go to university in Poland. Remember: she won prizes in both physics and chemistry.

**Scene 17 — Message + call to action**
[slowly] Marie's story teaches us: if one door is closed, find another way to learn. Want more practice? Go to brightlivesenglish.com for flashcards, a worksheet and a quiz. Please subscribe. See you in the next true story!

**Scene 18 — End card**
Logo on cream (no voiceover), music only, 4–5 seconds. Keep from EP03.

## Fact check

| Claim in script | Source fact |
|---|---|
| Born Maria Skłodowska in Warsaw, Poland, in 1867 | Born 7 November 1867, Warsaw (then in the Russian Empire) (NobelPrize.org; Britannica) |

| Studied at the secret Flying University | Studied at the clandestine "Flying University" (Britannica; NobelPrize.org) |
| Worked about five years as a teacher for families; helped her sister study in Paris | Worked as a governess/tutor for about five years and helped pay for her sister Bronisława's medical studies in Paris (NobelPrize.org; Britannica) |
| Moved to Paris in 1891 and went to university | Enrolled at the Sorbonne in 1891 (NobelPrize.org) |
| Little money, often cold and hungry | Lived in a small attic room on very little money (Britannica; NobelPrize.org stories) |
| Studied both physics and mathematics; one of the best students | Licence in physics 1893 (first in her class) and in mathematics 1894 (Britannica) |
| Met Pierre Curie; married in 1895 | Married Pierre Curie, 26 July 1895 (NobelPrize.org) |
| Worked in an old, cold shed | Their laboratory was a converted shed at the School of Physics and Chemistry (Britannica) |
| Discovered two new elements in 1898; polonium named after Poland | Announced polonium (July 1898) and radium (December 1898) (NobelPrize.org; Britannica) |
| 1903 Nobel Prize in physics with Pierre and Henri Becquerel; first woman to win a Nobel Prize | Nobel Prize in Physics 1903, shared with Pierre Curie and Henri Becquerel; first woman laureate (NobelPrize.org) |
| Pierre died in a street accident in 1906 | Killed in a street accident in Paris, 19 April 1906 (NobelPrize.org; Britannica) |
| First woman to teach as a professor at her university | In 1906 she became the first woman to teach at the Sorbonne / first woman professor at the University of Paris (Britannica) |
| Second Nobel Prize, in chemistry, 1911 | Nobel Prize in Chemistry 1911 (NobelPrize.org) |
| Only person with Nobel Prizes in both physics and chemistry | "She was and still is the only person to be awarded Nobel Prizes in two scientific categories" (NobelPrize.org) |
